# Alignment Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Declaring "we're aligned" with no decision capture.** Verbal agreement with no written decision, owners, or dates re-litigates the next day. Send a summary within 24 hours with a review checkpoint.
- **Treating stakeholders as a monolith.** Different people optimize for different things (risk vs growth vs quality). If those principles stay implicit, they argue past each other.
- **Springing the ask in the live meeting.** No pre-brief means the first "no" happens in front of everyone, and hidden vetoes surface too late. Pre-brief the decider and risk functions early.
- **Drifting into implementation trivia.** When "how" debates stand in for "what/why", restate the decision, the vital question, and the criteria, and park implementation with owners.
- **Trying to win the politics.** Manipulating someone into a yes isn't alignment; it collapses on contact with reality. Optimize for criteria and evidence, not for the vote.
- **Everything-matters criteria.** More than 3–7 criteria, or criteria with no real tradeoff, means the decision isn't decision-ready.

## The alignment target

Alignment means one of three things — inform, align, or decide — and the pack must say which, with a date. The decision or commitment must fit in one sentence. If it can't, you don't yet have a decision; do problem definition first.

## Decoding how leaders think (principles, not opinions)

Understand what a stakeholder optimizes for, not just what they said. Translate feedback into 3–7 decision principles (e.g., "must feel like the future", "minimize enterprise risk") plus the evidence they trust. The test: you can predict their top questions and what would change their mind. For each objection, ask "in what world does this make sense?"

Question prompts to decode principles:

- "What's the most important thing you optimize for in decisions like this?"
- "What risks are you most worried about here?"
- "What would make this an obvious yes for you?"
- "What evidence would change your mind?"

## Anchoring on user value + constraints

Open with a simple, user-centric vital question and keep returning to it; if the meeting derails into implementation trivia, the vital question recenters it. Earn trust by making business constraints explicit — legal/compliance, monetization, GTM, security, reliability — so risk-focused stakeholders see their concerns reflected accurately.

Prompts:

- "How does this make our users/customers meaningfully better off?"
- "What user pain does this remove, and how will we know?"
- "Let's agree on criteria first; we'll evaluate options against them."
- "Initial discomfort is normal — let's focus on what works for users and the business."

## Reading alignment signals

During pre-briefs and meetings, capture signals: what caused visible interest (energy, faster follow-ups, deeper questions); what caused disengagement (dead eyes, topic switching, vague approval); and which words or frames stakeholders repeated back (evidence it landed). Use the pre-brief notes sheet in [TEMPLATES.md](TEMPLATES.md) to log signals and adjust messaging.

## Common failure modes (and fixes)

- **"We're aligned" then re-litigation:** no written decision capture, dissent unrecorded, owners unclear. Fix: decision summary within 24 hours plus a review checkpoint.
- **Stakeholders argue past each other:** implicit, conflicting decision principles. Fix: write down principles and criteria; ask "in what world does this objection make sense?"
- **Meetings drift into implementation:** no clear decision target or criteria. Fix: restate decision, vital question, and criteria; park "how" with owners.
- **Surprise blockers appear late:** missing stakeholder map, no pre-brief loop, hidden vetoes. Fix: map by function; pre-brief decider and risk functions early.
