# Coaching PMs Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Principles (the checks behind the stance)

1. **Coach, don't rescue.** Default to questions that build the PM's independent judgment; give the answer only when risk or time demands it.
   *Check:* over a cycle, the same decision type is not repeatedly handed to you — the PM brings options and a recommendation, not just the problem.
2. **Define good before you diagnose.** Agree on what a good PM means in this context (behaviors + Bloom depth) before assessing anyone.
   *Check:* for each competency you can name observable behaviors and an artifact that shows it.
3. **Assess from evidence, not impressions.** Map real artifacts and decisions to competencies; label uncertainty where evidence is thin.
   *Check:* every claimed gap has at least one concrete example behind it.
4. **Separate skill gaps from context gaps.** Coaching fixes skills; it cannot fix a missing mandate, absent data, or no authority.
   *Check:* before prescribing reps, you have ruled out (or named) the constraints that would make the reps pointless.
5. **Grow judgment through reps one level up.** Skill deepens by deliberately practicing the next Bloom level on real work, not by consuming more theory.
   *Check:* each rep forces the PM to operate one level above their current baseline (e.g., Apply → Analyze).
6. **Make progress visible and follow up.** Coaching is the primary job of product leadership because it is how you scale — but only if it compounds.
   *Check:* a tracker shows bets → reps → artifacts → review outcomes, reviewed on a cadence.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Being the answer machine.** Jumping to your solution trains dependency and stalls the PM's growth. Ask "what would you do if I weren't here?" and wait through the silence.
- **Advice before a shared definition of good.** Prescribing "be more strategic" without agreed behaviors gives the PM nothing to practice. Define the bar first.
- **Coaching a constraint as if it were a skill.** Running reps on "stakeholder alignment" when the real problem is no decision authority wastes the cycle. Remove or name the constraint.
- **Too many growth bets.** Four or five bets means nothing changes. Pick 1–3 with the biggest second-order effects.
- **Optimizing for activity, not outcomes.** "Do more research" is motion; "make a decision with evidence by Friday" is a rep. State targets as behaviors and artifacts.
- **Vibes-based assessment.** Ratings with no artifact behind them are opinions. Cite the PRD, the decision brief, the launch.
- **No follow-up.** Coaching that is a string of one-off conversations never compounds. Keep the tracker and review it.

## Competency areas (edit for your org)

- **Problem framing & insight** — JTBD, research, data, opportunity sizing.
- **Strategy & prioritization** — bets, sequencing, rationale, tradeoffs.
- **Execution & delivery** — planning, risk management, iteration, quality.
- **Stakeholder leadership** — alignment, decision-making, conflict, comms.
- **Product craft** — PRDs/specs, narratives, UX judgment, edge cases.
- **Business acumen** — unit economics, go-to-market, pricing, metrics.

For each competency, pick 1–2 anchor behaviors per Bloom level (observable actions or artifacts) so the ladder is concrete.

## Bloom's Taxonomy for skill depth

Treat each competency as a ladder of thinking depth (after Bangaly Kaba):

- **Knowledge:** can name concepts and vocabulary.
- **Comprehension:** can explain concepts in their own words.
- **Application:** can apply concepts on real work with support.
- **Analysis:** can break down tradeoffs and diagnose root causes.
- **Synthesis:** can create a new approach or strategy under constraints.
- **Evaluation:** can judge options, justify decisions, and teach others.

The depth ladder is what makes "get better at strategy" actionable — it names the specific rung to climb next.

## Assessment — evidence and gap types

Low-friction evidence sources:

- 2–5 recent artifacts (PRD, roadmap slice, decision brief, launch plan, postmortem).
- 1–2 stakeholder feedback summaries (anonymized).
- An optional structured PM self-assessment.

Separate the two gap types before prescribing:

- **Skill gap:** the PM doesn't yet know how to do the work.
- **Context gap:** unclear mandate, missing data, no authority, shifting priorities. Coaching won't fix this — surface it and remove the constraint.

## Choosing growth bets

- Pick the smallest number of bets that create the biggest second-order effects.
- Prefer bets that show up in multiple competencies — weak problem framing often drags prioritization, execution, and comms with it.
- State targets as behaviors and artifacts, not traits. Example: "Move problem framing from Apply → Analyze by consistently producing a 1-page opportunity assessment with explicit tradeoffs."

## Designing reps (Bloom-driven)

Design each bet with a stretch assignment (real work, real constraints), weekly reps (small, repeatable, 30–90 min), required artifacts, coach commitments (review, shadow, introductions), and a measurement signal (rubric + evidence).

Aim each rep one level above the current baseline:

- Current **Comprehension** → reps force **Application** (do the thing on real work).
- Current **Application** → reps force **Analysis** (explicit tradeoffs, root-cause thinking).
- Current **Analysis** → reps force **Synthesis/Evaluation** (new strategy + clear rationale, teach others).

## Coaching cadence & prompts

Good cadence pattern: weekly or biweekly 1:1s plus a lightweight async artifact-review loop — a predictable reps → feedback → adjust rhythm. Coaching is how product leadership scales (Marty Cagan), so protect the time.

Coaching prompts (pick a few):

- "What problem are we solving, and for whom? What evidence?"
- "What options did you consider? What tradeoffs did you reject?"
- "If this fails, why will it fail? What would you do differently?"
- "What would you do with half the time? Double the time?"
- "What is the smallest test or decision we can make this week?"

When to be directive (exceptions):

- Safety/compliance risk, major customer impact, or time-critical decisions.
- Repeated blind spots after coaching cycles — then reset expectations and structure.

## Follow-up & measurement

- Keep a simple tracker: bet → reps → artifacts → review outcomes.
- Review monthly: keep or adjust bets, remove constraints, celebrate visible progress, and hand back autonomy as capability grows.
- Measure by artifacts and behaviors, not effort or activity.
