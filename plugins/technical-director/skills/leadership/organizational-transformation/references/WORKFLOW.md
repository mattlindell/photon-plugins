# Transformation Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and failure modes for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Outcomes, not frameworks

- Treat frameworks as a toolbox, not a destination.
- Write 2-3 anti-goals (things you explicitly will not change yet) to reduce fear and scope creep.
- Sponsors should be able to state success as outcomes and behaviors, not "we implemented X."

## Diagnosing the operating model as a system

- Map reality - how decisions actually happen - not the org chart.
- Look for reinforcing loops that keep feature teams in place: annual planning + output metrics + centralized approvals.
- Watch the incentive gap: leaders say "outcomes" but reward output. That gap defeats the transformation before it starts.

## Thesis + framework hygiene

- Pick the smallest set of changes that creates leverage - often decision rights + team boundaries + discovery expectations.
- Define a "borrow list" vs "do not borrow" from popular frameworks to reduce cargo-culting.
- A model designed for a different product, architecture, or talent maturity won't transplant cleanly.

## Designing the target operating model

- Make it observable: specify required artifacts (problem briefs, discovery notes, decision logs).
- Define the Day 1 operating model: meetings, owners, escalation paths, and where truth lives.
- Pair new expectations with capability building - new roles without training/coaching just sets people up to fail.

## Nudge-first pilots (90 days)

- Prefer pilots that produce proof quickly (2-6 weeks) and are reversible.
- Use nudges: templates, rituals, coaching, and default paths rather than mandates.
- Pick pilots for leverage, not politics; a low-impact or doomed area teaches nothing.
- Build the learning loop up front, so insights actually change the plan.

## Roadmap (6-12 months)

- Sequence so capability building and decision rights precede structural change.
- Reserve capacity for transformation work; teams cannot do it "on top" of everything else.
- Avoid big-bang reorgs - in-flight work stalls and trust drops.

## Change + comms

- Communication is an operating mechanism: repeat, show examples, reinforce with rituals.
- Plan for resistance - objections often contain real risks; harvest them into mitigations.
- Leaders must model the new behaviors; teams follow incentives, not slides.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Framework as the goal.** "Adopt SAFe/Spotify" framed as success, instead of the outcomes it was supposed to produce.
- **Empowerment by announcement.** Declaring empowered teams while decision rights, incentives, and approvals stay exactly where they were.
- **Everything at once.** A big-bang rollout that overwhelms change capacity and triggers systemic rejection.
- **Vague diagnosis.** "It's a culture problem" with no concrete mechanism anyone can change.
- **Politics-picked pilots.** Choosing a safe or doomed area for optics rather than a high-leverage one that would create real proof.
- **Announce-then-silence.** One kickoff message and no reinforcement, so rumor and resistance fill the gap.
- **Unresourced transformation.** Expecting teams to transform on top of a full delivery load, with no reserved capacity.
