# Team Dynamics Framework for Scrum Teams

Combines Tuckman's stages of group development with psychological safety principles and scrum-specific interventions.
Development is non-linear — teams cycle between stages as membership and context change — and facilitation style must
match the current stage.

---

## Tuckman's Stages Applied to Scrum

Each stage below carries the quantitative ranges that place a team in it. Use the CV and psychological-safety ranges to
corroborate a maturity call from velocity and survey data.

### Forming

_Getting to know each other and the work._ Members work independently and defer to the Scrum Master; ceremonies feel
forced; velocity is low and erratic.

**Scrum Master style:** directing — teach the framework rigorously, set context, build trust.

| Metric                  | Forming range             |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Ceremony participation  | 60-80%                    |
| Velocity predictability | High volatility (CV >40%) |
| Psychological safety    | 2.0-3.5/5.0               |
| Conflict frequency      | Very low                  |

**Interventions:** team charter and working agreements, skill inventory, pairing/mobbing, intensive process education.

**Exit signals:** consistent ceremony attendance (>85%), members asking process questions, initial working agreements
established.

### Storming

_Working through differences._ Disagreements over technical approach and priorities surface; members question scrum
practices; velocity fluctuates.

**Scrum Master style:** coaching — guide conflict resolution without dictating solutions, guard psychological safety,
keep conflict productive.

| Metric               | Storming range       |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Ceremony engagement  | Variable (70-90%)    |
| Velocity volatility  | Moderate (CV 25-40%) |
| Psychological safety | 2.5-4.0/5.0          |
| Conflict frequency   | Moderate-high        |

**Interventions:** structured conflict-resolution sessions, retrospective deep-dives into team dynamics, individual
1:1s, working-agreement updates, external facilitation for significant conflicts.

**Exit signals:** conflicts addressed openly, participation becomes authentic, velocity begins to stabilize.

### Norming

_Establishing effective ways of working._ The team takes collective ownership, self-organizes process improvements, and
delivers more predictably.

**Scrum Master style:** supporting — step back, focus on external impediments, shield the team from disruption.

| Metric                 | Norming range    |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Ceremony effectiveness | 80-90%           |
| Velocity consistency   | Good (CV 15-25%) |
| Psychological safety   | 3.5-4.5/5.0      |
| Knowledge sharing      | High             |

**Interventions:** transfer ceremony facilitation to the team, focus on skill development, help the team define its own
success metrics, establish a continuous-improvement rhythm.

**Exit signals:** team facilitates its own ceremonies, proactively resolves impediments, sustains stable velocity, runs
high-quality retrospectives.

### Performing

_Delivering exceptional results._ The team self-manages, adapts quickly, innovates, and develops its own members.

**Scrum Master style:** delegating — minimal intervention; focus on long-term capability and organizational influence.

| Metric               | Performing range            |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Autonomy level       | High                        |
| Velocity excellence  | High + consistent (CV <15%) |
| Psychological safety | 4.0-5.0/5.0                 |
| Innovation frequency | Regular                     |

**Interventions:** introduce stretch goals, grow coaching/mentoring skills, facilitate teaching other teams, connect
team excellence to organizational goals.

### Adjourning

Project completion or team dissolution. Focus on a comprehensive final retrospective, documenting effective practices,
knowledge transfer to successor teams, and acknowledging the team's achievements.

## Psychological Safety

The belief that one can show vulnerability, ask questions, admit mistakes, and propose ideas without risk to self-image,
status, or career. Without it, teams cannot reach high performance.

### Building it across ceremonies

- **Standups:** model vulnerability; ask "Who needs help?" not "Any blockers?"; protect time so everyone speaks.
- **Planning:** no judgment for "wrong" estimates; safe to state realistic availability; team can push back on
  unrealistic commitments.
- **Reviews:** discuss what didn't work without blame; present a unified front to stakeholders; frame setbacks as
  learning.
- **Retrospectives:** focus on systems not individuals; ensure equal participation; what's said in retro stays in retro.

### Edmondson's 7-point scale

Administer anonymously; items 1, 3, and 5 are reverse-scored.

1. If you make a mistake on this team, it is often held against you.
2. Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues.
3. People on this team sometimes reject others for being different.
4. It is safe to take a risk on this team.
5. It is difficult to ask other members of this team for help.
6. No one on this team would deliberately act to undermine my efforts.
7. Working with members of this team, my unique skills and talents are valued and utilized.

Target index: >4.0/5.0. Re-survey monthly and track the trend, not the absolute score.

## Conflict Resolution

**Healthy conflict** is task-focused, solution-oriented, open, respectful, and resolves without festering. **Unhealthy
conflict** targets individuals, is zero-sum, goes underground as gossip, damages trust, and persists.

Resolution process:

1. **Detect early** — recurring retrospective themes, participation drops, velocity dips, 1:1 concerns.
2. **Assess** — map who is involved and affected, separate facts from interpretation, define what resolution looks like.
3. **Facilitate** — set ground rules, let each party state their view, find common ground, generate solutions
   collaboratively, capture clear commitments.
4. **Follow up** — support implementation, repair relationships, extract a prevention learning.

---

## Crisis Interventions

Escalate beyond stage-appropriate facilitation when these symptoms appear:

- **Performance regression** (sudden velocity/quality/satisfaction drops): run a health check and root-cause analysis,
  hold individual 1:1s, audit the process for systemic issues.
- **Psychological safety violation** (members withdrawing, avoiding risk, leaving): take immediate protective action,
  hold a team-wide discussion of safety principles, coach whoever violated safety, change the system that allowed it.
- **External pressure** (stress, process shortcuts, less collaboration): educate stakeholders on sustainable pace,
  negotiate scope and priorities, protect team capacity.

If scores plateau or regress for 2 consecutive sprints, treat it as a regression and escalate.

---

## Measurement Cadence

- **Weekly:** ceremony quality observation.
- **Sprint:** velocity and quality metrics; re-run the health scorer.
- **Monthly:** psychological safety pulse survey.
- **Quarterly:** full maturity re-assessment.

Combine quantitative signals (velocity, attendance, survey scores) with qualitative ones (observation notes,
retrospective themes) — neither alone places a team accurately.
