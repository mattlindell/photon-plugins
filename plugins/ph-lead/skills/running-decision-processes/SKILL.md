# Running Decision Processes

## Decide with clarity, then commit

Anchor every choice below to four ideas:

- **Match rigor to reversibility.** A two-way door deserves speed and a lightweight memo; a one-way door deserves explicit criteria, roles, and assumptions. Don't pay heavy process tax on reversible calls, and don't wing the irreversible ones.
- **One Decider, no hidden vetoes.** Name exactly who decides and who can block. Ambiguous decision rights are the source of decision drift and endless re-litigation.
- **Make assumptions explicit and testable.** Write down what must be true, so the choice can be reviewed and learned from later. Intuition you can't test can't improve.
- **The meeting's job is commitment, not consensus.** Decide, name the tradeoffs, assign an owner, set a review date. A decision no one communicates is a shadow decision.

## When to use / not

Use to run an end-to-end decision, prep and facilitate a decision meeting, clarify who decides, run a consultation loop, or log and schedule the review of a decision.

Do **not** use for: deciding **what problem to solve** — do problem definition first; **prioritizing across many opportunities** — that's a roadmap/prioritization workflow; a "decision" that's really a **status update** or routine coordination — use an operating cadence; or a **personal, legal, or HR** matter — escalate to humans and domain experts.

## Branch A — Run the full decision process

The spine for a cross-functional, high-stakes, or one-way-door decision.

1. **Classify and frame.** Call it a one-way door (hard to reverse) or two-way door (reversible), set the rigor (light/standard/heavy) and a timebox, and write the decision in one sentence: "We are deciding **X** by **date** to achieve **Y**." Heuristics: [references/WORKFLOW.md](references/WORKFLOW.md).
   **Done when:** a stakeholder can restate the decision in one sentence without adding qualifiers, and the rigor matches the reversibility.
2. **Run the historian pass.** Reconstruct what was tried before and why it failed; surface baggage and hidden constraints so they don't become late vetoes. Keep facts, assumptions, and opinions labeled separately.
   **Done when:** prior decisions and known constraints are captured, sorted into facts vs. assumptions.
3. **Generate options and criteria; log assumptions.** Define 2–4 viable options (include "do nothing" if apt) and the criteria that actually drive the choice, and make each option's key assumptions explicit and testable. Matrix template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).
   **Done when:** each option carries 2–3 explicit assumptions and the criteria reflect real tradeoffs, not a wish list.
4. **Set decision rights, consult, decide, and close the loop.** Assign a RAPID/DACI/RACI model with exactly one Decider and explicit veto rules (Branch C), run a consultation loop if the stakes warrant it (Branch D), then decide, log the rationale, communicate, and set a review date (Branch E). Pass [references/CHECKLISTS.md](references/CHECKLISTS.md) / [references/RUBRIC.md](references/RUBRIC.md) and close with **Risks / Open questions / Next steps**.
   **Done when:** the decision, owner, and review date are unambiguous, the team knows what changes tomorrow, and the checklist passes.

Deliverable: a decision pack — brief/pre-read, options + criteria matrix, decision rights, decision log entry, comms, and a review plan.

## Branch B — Prep & run a decision meeting

The workhorse. You have a decision to make soon and need to walk in ready to reach commitment — not design a rigorous program.

1. **Draft the pre-read.** State the decision, deadline, constraints, the 2–4 options, and a recommendation if you have one. Brief template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).
2. **Build the agenda.** Restate the decision, review options + assumptions, surface disagreement and what would change minds, decide, then next steps + comms + review date — timeboxed to 30–60 min.
3. **Plan the close.** Know who the Decider is going in, and how you'll capture the decision, owner, and tradeoffs before anyone leaves.

**Done when:** a one-page pre-read + agenda exists, the Decider is named, and the meeting ends with a logged decision, owner, and review date.

## Branch C — Clarify decision rights

The debate is stuck because nobody knows who decides. Pick a model (RAPID/DACI/RACI), assign roles so there is exactly **one Decider** (or a defined decision body), and make veto power explicit — including what requires Legal/Security/Finance sign-off and what the default is if someone doesn't respond by the deadline. Template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).

**Done when:** every role is assigned, there is one Decider, and veto vs. input is written down with a no-response default.

## Branch D — Consultation (curiosity) loop

Gather contextual advice before committing. Curate 8–12 people (domain experts + context-aware peers + those who'll be impacted) and ask specific, rationale-forcing questions — "pick your top 2 and why," "what's the #1 missing consideration," "what would change your mind?" Capture inputs and dissent; update the options and assumptions. Outreach template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).

**Done when:** inputs are specific and actionable, disagreement is recorded rather than smoothed over, and the matrix/assumptions are updated.

## Branch E — Log & review a decision

Capture what was decided and set up the learning loop. Write a decision log entry — decision, rationale, tradeoffs, assumptions, owner, and what would trigger a revisit — and schedule a review that compares outcomes against the assumptions. Template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).

**Done when:** the log entry is complete and a dated review with a measurement plan exists ("we'll revisit" with no date means never).

## Examples

- *"We need to decide whether to sunset Feature X by March 15 — run a RAPID process and draft the announcement."* → **Branch A**: classification, options/criteria matrix, RAPID roles, decision log, comms, and a review plan.
- *"We're split on build vs. buy for analytics and it's a one-way door — I have the options, help me run the meeting Thursday."* → **Branch B**: pre-read, timeboxed agenda, named Decider, and a logged decision with a review date.
- *"Help me decide if I should change careers."* → out of scope: this skill is for organizational decisions; suggest a personal decision framework or a coach instead.
