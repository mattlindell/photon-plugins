# Managing-Up Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Principles (the checks behind the stance)

1. **Partnership, not complaint.** Treat your manager as a teammate with unique leverage; make it easy for them to help you.
   *Check:* you have a consistent update cadence and clear asks instead of surprise escalations.
2. **Over-communicate context and trade-offs upward.** When you surface a problem, include the trade-offs, constraints, and what you recommend.
   *Check:* leaders rarely say "why didn't I know this sooner?" or "I don't understand what's at stake."
3. **Use leaders as resources, with specificity.** Translate blockers into a concrete action the leader can take — decide, sponsor, unblock, introduce, prioritize.
   *Check:* your asks are time-bounded, binary when possible, and easy to say yes or no to.
4. **Escalate earlier than feels comfortable, but not noisily.** Escalate while changing course is still possible; don't wait until the outcome is already determined.
   *Check:* escalations include options + a recommendation and land before the deadline cliff.
5. **Boundaries protect outcomes.** Push back on unreasonable demands by anchoring on outcomes and proposing alternatives.
   *Check:* you have a written working agreement and fewer "random 10pm meeting" incidents.
6. **Earn the seat before you're invited.** Show business-level thinking via crisp POVs, pre-reads, and trade-off framing; be useful in the forums you can access.
   *Check:* you're increasingly pulled into higher-level conversations and asked for input earlier.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Raw problem, no recommendation.** Dumping a problem on your manager makes them do your thinking. Bring 2–3 options and say which you'd pick.
- **Silent until the cliff.** Waiting until the deadline to escalate removes their ability to help. Escalate while course-correction is still possible.
- **Status theater.** A wall of activity with no decisions, risks, or asks wastes the reader. Lead with what needs a decision.
- **Vague asks.** "Can you help with X?" is unactionable. Make it binary, time-bound, and name the exact action.
- **Boundaries as complaints.** "I'm overloaded" invites nothing. Anchor on the outcome at risk and propose the trade-off.
- **Escalation noise.** Escalating everything trains leaders to tune you out. Reserve it for real triggers.
- **Managing up as impression management.** Optimizing for looking good over being useful gets seen through. Earn the seat by being useful, not visible.

## Channels — "what goes where"

Keep each type of communication in its home so nothing lands as a surprise drive-by:

- **Async weekly update:** progress vs plan, risks, decisions needed, asks, trade-offs.
- **1:1:** priorities, decision-making, coaching, alignment, relationship and feedback.
- **Decision memo / pre-read:** high-stakes choices, trade-offs, alignment ahead of meetings.
- **Urgent escalation path:** time-critical risks — a clear trigger list plus "what I need from you".

## Manager profile — validation prompts

Use these in a 1:1 to confirm your draft profile without sounding odd:

- "When you think about our org's success this quarter, what are the top 2–3 things you're optimizing for?"
- "How do you prefer I bring you issues — options first, or raw info first?"
- "What kinds of decisions do you want pulled to you vs handled at my level?"
- "What does a good weekly update look like for you: detail level, format, timing?"

## Weekly update — make it skimmable

- Put the ask near the top, or clearly in its own section.
- Use "no reply required unless…" to reduce burden.
- Avoid long narratives; link to deeper docs for detail.
- Always include trade-offs when outcomes look worse than expected.
- Send on a predictable day/time so it becomes a rhythm, not a nag.

## Ask ladder (light to heavy)

1. **FYI:** "No response needed; keeping you in the loop."
2. **Nudge:** "If you disagree, please tell me by <date>."
3. **Unblock:** "Please remove blocker X by doing Y."
4. **Sponsor:** "Please signal priority to <team/person> so we can proceed."
5. **Decision:** "Choose A or B by <date>; my recommendation is A because…"

## Escalation timing

Escalate when any trigger fires and course-correction is still possible:

- Decision rights are unclear or above your level.
- A timeline cliff is approaching and the current path misses it.
- A cross-team dependency is stalled.
- The same blocker has persisted past N days.
- Customer, revenue, or brand risk is emerging.

Bring options + a recommendation every time — never a naked problem, never a fait accompli.

## Boundary scripts (short patterns)

State the boundary, then propose an alternative:

- **Late-night meeting request:** "I can't do 10pm tonight. I can do 8am tomorrow, or send a written update now — what's better?"
- **Urgency inflation:** "If this is urgent, we should drop something else. Which priority should we deprioritize to make room?"
- **Scope creep:** "Adding this will push the date or reduce quality. Here are the options; which trade-off do you want?"

## Pre-wiring exec forums

Before a leadership meeting, walk the key people through:

- a 1–2 sentence TL;DR,
- the decision you want,
- the two main trade-offs,
- who you've already aligned with (and who you haven't).

## Influence / seat at the table

- Be useful in the rooms you already reach before angling for new ones.
- Share crisp POV notes and pre-reads that make others' decisions easier.
- Anchor contributions at the business level — outcomes and trade-offs, not task status.

## Retro prompts (end of 4-week pilot)

- "Where did we still get surprised?"
- "Which updates were too noisy or too thin?"
- "Where did escalation happen too late?"
- "Did we get faster decisions? More autonomy? Better alignment?"
