# Stakeholder Alignment

## Alignment is commitments, not vibes

Anchor every choice below to four ideas:

- **Commitments, not vibes.** Never end an alignment meeting without a decision, owners, and dates. If no one can say what changes tomorrow, nothing was aligned — it will re-litigate the next day.
- **No surprises.** Pre-brief the decider and likely blockers 1:1 before the live meeting. Nobody should meet the core ask for the first time in the room; that's how silent vetoes form.
- **Principles over opinions.** Translate each stakeholder's feedback into the decision principles they optimize for. You should be able to answer "in what world does their view make sense?" and predict their top questions.
- **Anchor on user value; respect real constraints.** Open with one vital question grounded in user value, and make business constraints (legal, security, margin, reliability) explicit so risk-minded stakeholders see their concerns reflected accurately.

## When to use / not

Use to run an alignment campaign, prep a single stakeholder pre-brief, facilitate a decision meeting, or write the lock-in comms after a decision.

Do **not** use when: there's no decision or commitment you can name in one sentence (do problem definition first); the ask is "make them agree with me" or "win the politics" — refuse manipulation and reframe to an evidence-based process, or route to a manager/HR if it's a relationship or performance issue; you only need a polished deck; or the matter is interpersonal/HR/legal and needs specialist counsel.

## Branch A — Campaign

The full flow. Produce a shareable alignment pack and run it to a committed decision.

1. **Name the target.** Classify the goal (inform / align / decide), state the decision in one sentence, set a date, and define what "done" looks like. Template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).
   **Done when:** you can finish "after alignment, stakeholders will commit to ___ by ___."
2. **Map stakeholders + decision rights.** Identify decider, approvers, influencers, implementers, blockers, and silent-veto risks. Mark who must not be surprised.
   **Done when:** every critical function is either included or explicitly out of scope, and the decider is named.
3. **Decode how they think + anchor the narrative.** Convert feedback into 3–7 decision principles per key stakeholder and note what evidence persuades them; draft the vital question, user value, why now, and the unavoidable tradeoffs. Heuristics and question prompts: [references/WORKFLOW.md](references/WORKFLOW.md).
   **Done when:** a skeptic can't dismiss the proposal as "nice to have" without disputing a stated assumption, and you can predict each stakeholder's top questions.
4. **Set evaluation criteria.** Create 3–7 criteria tied to stakeholder principles that force real tradeoffs; set the expectation that the right answer may feel uncomfortable at first.
   **Done when:** criteria are few, mutually meaningful, and mapped to stakeholder principles.
5. **Run the pre-brief loop (no surprises).** Meet key stakeholders 1:1, observe what lands and what causes dead eyes, capture objections, and keep a change log as you revise. Per-stakeholder branch: **B**.
   **Done when:** no major stakeholder sees the core ask for the first time in the live meeting.
6. **Decide + lock.** Facilitate the meeting (branch **C**) and send the lock-in comms (branch **D**). Then run [references/CHECKLISTS.md](references/CHECKLISTS.md), score with [references/RUBRIC.md](references/RUBRIC.md), and close with **Risks / Open questions / Next steps**.
   **Done when:** the decision, rationale, tradeoffs, owners, dates, and revisit trigger are captured and communicated.

## Branch B — Pre-brief a stakeholder

The workhorse. Prep a single 1:1 pre-brief, or decode one stakeholder you're stuck on. Notes sheet: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md); question prompts: [references/WORKFLOW.md](references/WORKFLOW.md).

1. **Decode their principles.** What do they optimize for, which risks scare them, what evidence do they trust, what would make this an obvious yes? Answer "in what world does their objection make sense?"
2. **Set the pre-brief goal** (secure support / surface objections / validate criteria / confirm constraints) and plan the one ask.
3. **Rehearse for signals.** Note what you expect to land, and what you'll do if you hit dead eyes or a hidden objection.

**Done when:** you can state this stakeholder's top objection, the evidence that would move them, and the single commitment you'll ask for — before you walk in.

## Branch C — Decision meeting

Facilitate the alignment/decision meeting. Open with the alignment target and vital question, walk options against criteria, confirm the decision and commitments, and record dissent. Agenda and live decision-capture block: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).

**Done when:** everyone leaves knowing what was decided, why, what changes tomorrow, who owns what, and dissent is recorded rather than smoothed over.

## Branch D — Lock-in comms

Send a crisp summary to all stakeholders (including those not in the room) that prevents re-litigation. Template: [references/TEMPLATES.md](references/TEMPLATES.md).

**Done when:** the comms states the decision, rationale, tradeoffs, owners, dates, and what would trigger a revisit — and lands within ~24 hours of the decision.

## Examples

- *"We need exec alignment to pause Feature A for 6 weeks for reliability; the CTO worries about morale and the CRO about pipeline."* → **Branch A**: alignment brief, stakeholder map capturing CTO/CRO principles, a pre-brief sequence, a meeting that ends in decision capture, and 24-hour comms.
- *"I have a pre-brief with Legal tomorrow and they always block launches."* → **Branch B**: decode Legal's risk principles, anticipate the top objection, decide the single commitment to ask for, and prep the evidence that would move them.
- *"My stakeholder just hates me — write a message that makes them agree."* → out of scope: refuse manipulation; reframe to an evidence-based alignment process, or route to a manager/HR if it's a relationship issue.
