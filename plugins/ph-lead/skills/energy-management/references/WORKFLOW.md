# Energy Management Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Principles (the checks behind the stance)

1. **Manage energy, not time.** Redesign the week around where your best energy goes, not just how many hours are booked.
   *Check:* deep/high-leverage work lands in high-energy windows; admin lands in low-energy ones.
2. **Design systems, not willpower.** Energy is protected by the calendar, delegation, and norms - not by trying harder.
   *Check:* every recovery action is a scheduled calendar object, not an intention.
3. **Recovery is a performance input.** Buffers and breaks are how sustained output happens, not a reward for surviving the crunch.
   *Check:* high-load blocks have buffers; there is a written low-energy-day protocol.
4. **Persistent low energy is a risk signal.** A slow slide toward no-joy or exhaustion needs a plan or a referral, not a pep talk.
   *Check:* the plan distinguishes a workload problem (adjust scope) from a burnout/safety problem (route to resources).
5. **Protect the team's energy too.** When helping with a report, adjust workload and scope; coach, do not diagnose.
   *Check:* a report's plan names concrete workload levers and an escalation path.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Hustle as a badge of honor.** Glorifying overload ("I ran on four hours") normalizes the thing you are trying to fix. Model sustainable pace, especially as a leader.
- **Treating rest as a reward.** "I'll recover after this ships" guarantees the recovery never gets scheduled. Book it first.
- **Diagnosing or playing therapist.** Naming clinical burnout, insomnia, or anxiety is out of scope. Redirect to professional help and adjust the work you actually control.
- **Ignoring the slow decline.** A drift from 7s to 4s over a month is a burnout signal even if no single week looked alarming. Watch the trend, not the data point.
- **Optimizing an overloaded (or unsafe) situation.** If the load is structurally impossible, no schedule fixes it - change scope, staffing, or deadlines instead of buffering harder.
- **Offloading without guardrails.** Delegation without decision rights and escalation triggers just moves the anxiety and creates rework.
- **Too many experiments at once.** Running five changes at once makes results unattributable. Cap the pilot at 2-4.
- **Confusing busy with high-leverage.** A packed calendar is not proof of impact. Bucket honestly.

## Energy signals - turning "I feel drained" into patterns

Aim: convert a vague feeling into observable triggers you can act on.

- Use an after-action prompt: right after a meeting or task, ask "energized or sapped?" and note *why*.
- If calendar detail is missing, start with the top 10 interactions from last week rather than waiting for perfect data.
- Push past vague labels ("people", "work") to specific triggers: format, topic, audience, prep level, uncertainty, conflict, context switching.

## The levers (attach one to every drain)

- **Eliminate:** stop attending or remove a low-value meeting entirely.
- **Delegate:** transfer ownership *with* decision rights, guardrails, and escalation triggers.
- **Redesign:** change the format - async pre-read, named decision owner, shorter default.
- **Time-shift:** move high-load work into a high-energy window.
- **Batch + buffer:** cluster similar work and add transition time between blocks.
- **Prepare:** a 5-minute pre-brief to cut anxiety and uncertainty.
- **Recover:** a micro-break scheduled after a high-load interaction.

Rule of thumb: a drain without a named lever, owner, and next action is just a complaint.

## Calendar buckets - zone of genius vs energy tax

Bucket a representative week; the goal is to shrink the bottom buckets, not to look busy.

- **Zone of genius / high leverage:** strategic thinking, coaching, key decisions, creative work.
- **Necessary but neutral:** admin, routine status, predictable ops.
- **Energy drains:** meetings with unclear purpose, constant interruptions, conflict without structure.
- **Recovery/admin:** buffers, breaks, transitions, decompression.

Then estimate current vs target mix and pick the 3-5 biggest offenders to cut or redesign. Failure mode: treating "busy" as "high leverage."

## Zone-of-genius expansion (stop/delegate/automate/defer)

Change the mix, not just cope with it.

- Build a "bottom bucket" list and commit to removing 2 items in the next 2 weeks.
- For delegation, specify outcomes, constraints, escalation triggers, and review points - never a bare handoff.

## Energy-aligned week + meeting hygiene

Redesign so the best plan is also the easiest plan to follow.

- Default to 25/50 minute meetings unless there is a reason not to.
- Require a purpose, agenda, and named decision owner.
- Batch 1:1s and low-stakes meetings; move complex topics to an async pre-read so the meeting is for debate and decision.
- Protect at least one deep-work block in a high-energy window.
- Add buffers after high-load meetings (conflict, performance, customer escalations).
- Failure mode: designing a schedule that assumes perfect compliance from everyone else - build in slack.

## Recovery, transitions, and the low-energy day

Build resilience without moralizing.

- Treat recovery as a calendar object (buffer blocks), not an intention.
- Micro-recovery menu: short walk/movement, eyes-off-screen breathing, water/snack reset, quick journaling. For remote work, optional sensory or movement supports (standing, walking calls, doodling/fidgeting) reduce cognitive load - no medical claims.
- Low-energy-day protocol: define the "minimum viable day" (1-2 non-negotiable outcomes), what gets postponed, and a short stakeholder message.
- Failure mode: over-indexing on personal optimization while ignoring workload and role design.

## Boundaries + escalation (workload vs burnout)

- Energy/joy check-ins spot risk; they do not diagnose.
- If the driver is workload, adjust scope, priorities, or expectations - and write it down.
- If someone (you or a report) reports persistent burnout symptoms, a safety risk, or a mental-health crisis, follow company policy and encourage professional resources / EAP. That is the escalation path, not a lever.

## Supporting a report's energy (team lens)

- Name the observed pattern (missed deadlines, withdrawal, no-joy) as behavior, not a verdict.
- Coach: ask what is draining them and what one change would help most this week.
- Pull the workload levers you control (scope, deadline, reassignment, backup) before anything else.
- Set a follow-up and an explicit escalation path if it does not improve.

## Measurement + iteration (2-week pilot)

Keep measures lightweight and attributable:

- End-of-day energy rating (1-5).
- "Drain count" per day (number of high-drain events).
- Deep-work blocks per week.
- Meeting hours per week and a quick "meeting satisfaction" read.

Decision rules after two weeks:

- **Keep:** improves energy with acceptable trade-offs.
- **Modify:** helps but causes new problems; adjust the lever.
- **Stop:** not worth it; revert and try a different lever.
