# Team Culture Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Principles (the checks behind the stance)

1. **Articulate before you invent.** Describe the culture that already exists (what it rewards and punishes) before deciding what to keep or change.
   *Check:* the team can recognize themselves in the snapshot without feeling caricatured.
2. **Listen first when you are new.** Prioritize listening over coming in swinging.
   *Check:* you can cite 5–10 concrete examples from lived experience before proposing changes.
3. **Culture is a decision-making system.** Values must become decision rules people use when the boss is not around.
   *Check:* for each principle you can answer, "What decision does this change?"
4. **Keep progress visible.** Maintain creative ambition and remove sacred cows that block evolution; stagnation usually looks like "nothing changes."
   *Check:* rituals include a recurring "progress visible" moment (demo, shipping review, customer-impact review).
5. **Reinforce via coaching, not policing.** Prefer skill development and modeling over compliance.
   *Check:* the rollout names who coaches whom and how feedback is practiced.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Announcing the doc and calling it done.** A published culture code with no rituals, hiring hooks, or modeling changes nothing. Reinforcement is the work.
- **Inventing aspirational values.** Values the team does not live read as corporate wallpaper. Start from what already works and make gaps explicit.
- **Generic "be excellent" principles.** If a principle has no do/don't and no decision rule, it cannot guide behavior. Make it observable.
- **"Culture fit" as a personality test.** Fit language screens for sameness. Define it as observable behavior aligned to principles.
- **Big-bang rollout.** Changing everything at once overwhelms and stalls. Pilot 1–2 rituals or norms and iterate.
- **Policing instead of coaching.** Catching people out breeds silence. Tie the moment to the principle and coach the behavior.
- **Skipping the listening tour when new.** Prescribing before you understand burns trust you will need later.

## Culture snapshot — what to capture

Capture these in 1–2 pages:

- **Stated culture:** what leadership says matters (values, principles, slogans).
- **Lived culture:** what actually gets rewarded and punished in practice.
- **Decision patterns:** how decisions get made (data, hierarchy, loudest voice, consensus).
- **Conflict patterns:** how disagreement works (avoidance, escalation, productive debate).
- **Ownership:** where responsibility is clear vs fuzzy.
- **Psychological-safety signals:** who speaks, who stays silent, what happens after mistakes.
- **Sacred cows:** taboos and untouchables that constrain progress.

## Listening tour (two weeks)

- 30–45 min 1:1s across roles and levels, or small-group listening sessions.
- Synthesize themes without naming individuals — protect safety.

Listening prompts (copy/paste):

1. "What's the best part of working on this team?"
2. "When have you felt proud of our work? What enabled it?"
3. "When do we move too slowly? Why?"
4. "What's hard to say out loud here?"
5. "What behaviors get rewarded, explicitly or implicitly?"
6. "What should we stop doing immediately?"
7. "If you led this team, what 1–2 changes would you make first?"

## Diagnosing focus areas

Pick **2–4** focus areas max. Common ones:

- Decision speed and clarity
- Feedback quality and conflict norms
- Ownership and accountability
- Ambition, creativity, customer obsession (countering stagnation)
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Psychological safety and learning from mistakes

For each, capture: current pattern (with examples), root causes (structure, incentives, norms, leadership behavior), new behaviors to reinforce, and a leading indicator observable within weeks.

## Writing the culture code

Keep to **3–7** principles. For each: a short memorable name, a definition, do/don't behaviors, decision rules for common dilemmas, anti-patterns (what this is not), and signals of a healthy version.

## Norms and rituals

Design norms for:

- **Communication:** async vs sync, response expectations, documentation.
- **Meetings:** default agendas, facilitation, "no surprises," decision capture.
- **Decisions:** DACI/RAPID/DRI, disagree-and-commit, escalation.
- **Feedback and conflict:** how to challenge, how to give feedback, repair after conflict.

Design each ritual with: name, purpose (which principle it reinforces), cadence and duration, owner/facilitator, participants, standard agenda, and outputs (what gets written down).

## Rollout and reinforcement

Use multiple channels, not one announcement:

- **Onboarding:** teach principles and norms in week one.
- **Hiring:** interview signals aligned to principles; avoid vague "culture fit."
- **Coaching:** peer/craft coaches and regular growth conversations.
- **Recognition:** reward behaviors, not just outcomes.
- **Moments:** offsites, planning cycles, retros, launch reviews.

## Measurement (lightweight)

- Monthly pulse questions on safety, clarity, ownership, decision speed.
- Ritual adherence — are we actually doing the rituals?
- Qualitative examples — two wins and one miss per month.
