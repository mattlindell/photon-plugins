# 1:1 Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics and defaults for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Principles (the checks behind the stance)

1. **Coach, don't bottleneck.** Default to coaching questions before giving answers; use advisor mode only when risk or time demands it.
   *Check:* over four weeks, the same problem type is not repeatedly escalated without the report trying options first.
2. **Status has a different home.** Don't let 1:1s become the primary status channel; keep a separate async or team ritual.
   *Check:* 1:1 notes show decisions, risks, growth, and unblocking — not a replay of ticket lists.
3. **Career gets dedicated time.** Schedule deeper career sessions distinct from weekly tactical 1:1s.
   *Check:* each report has 1–3 written growth bets and a review checkpoint on the calendar.
4. **Use a barbell for time.** Combine fewer, higher-quality relationship catch-ups with timely urgent topical meetings.
   *Check:* calendar load drops while time-to-escalate improves (fewer "we should have talked sooner" moments).
5. **Recovery/joy is a performance input.** Ask about energy; treat persistent "no joy" as a risk signal that needs a plan.
   *Check:* there is a lightweight activation plan and documented boundaries/escalation.
6. **In crisis, listen first.** After major change, run a listening-only 1:1 focused on thoughts and feelings; skip status and problem-solving.
   *Check:* the report can say they felt heard; any actions are limited to support and clarity.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **You're doing most of the talking.** If you use more than ~half the airtime, it's your meeting, not theirs. Ask, then wait through the silence.
- **Skipping the 1:1 when busy.** The weeks you're too busy to meet are the weeks it matters most. Reschedule; don't drop.
- **Solving instead of coaching.** Jumping to the answer trains dependency. Ask "what would you do if I weren't here?"
- **Kitchen-sinking feedback.** Saving up a pile of issues for one meeting overwhelms. Give feedback close to the event, one or two items at a time.
- **Status crowds out everything.** If the whole meeting is a ticket replay, move status async and reclaim the time for coaching, feedback, and growth.

## Channels — "what goes where"

Use this to keep 1:1s from turning into status meetings:

- **Team ritual / async channel:** project status, sprint updates, cross-functional coordination, broad announcements.
- **1:1:** coaching, decision support, feedback, career growth, wellbeing, sensitive blockers, relationship building.
- **Urgent topical meeting:** time-sensitive decisions, incident response, immediate escalation.
- **Skip-level:** signal gathering, coaching at a distance, culture/health checks — not bypassing the manager.

## Cadence heuristics

Default starting points:

- **New manager ↔ new report:** 45–60 min weekly for 4–8 weeks, then reassess.
- **Stable relationship:** 30–45 min biweekly.
- **Senior / independent report:** 30–45 min every 2–4 weeks + ad-hoc topical meetings.
- **Relationship under stress / major change:** temporarily increase cadence and add a listening session.

Barbell patterns:

- Replace some standing 1:1s with a monthly relationship catch-up (walk/coffee; no laptop).
- Keep a fast path: a 15–30 min topical meeting within 48 hours for urgent issues.

### New report — the first 90 days

A new report's 1:1 arc is front-loaded, then tapers:

- **Weeks 1–4:** weekly, longer. Cover expectations, how they like to work and receive feedback, how you'll operate together, and early wins. Write down the working agreement.
- **Weeks 5–8:** weekly, shifting from onboarding logistics to real work, blockers, and first feedback loops.
- **Weeks 9–12:** confirm the steady-state cadence, run the first Life Story career session, and set the first growth bet.

## Shared-doc guidance

- Keep one doc per manager-report pair (or per quarter), with a running topic backlog.
- Both people add agenda items before the meeting.
- End each meeting with explicit next steps: owner + due date (or next checkpoint).

Shared vs private notes:

- Prefer shared notes by default — it builds trust.
- If you keep private notes, don't store sensitive personal info; keep it factual and job-relevant.

## Coaching toolkit (mini scripts)

When a report brings a problem:

1. "What outcome do you want?"
2. "What options have you considered?"
3. "What would you do if I weren't available?"
4. "What's the riskiest assumption here?"
5. "What decision do you need to make, and by when?"

When you disagree:

- "Talk me through your reasoning."
- "What data would change your mind?"
- "What's the smallest test we can run this week?"

When to be directive (exceptions):

- Safety, legal/HR policy, ethical issues.
- Time-critical incidents.
- Repeated patterns where expectations were already clearly set.

## Tracking signals over time

A single 1:1 is a data point; the value is in the trend. Skim the shared doc before each meeting and watch for drift:

- **Energy:** a slow slide from 7s to 4s over a month is a burnout signal even if no single week looked alarming.
- **Escalation pattern:** the same problem type coming back repeatedly means coaching isn't sticking — change the approach or set clearer expectations.
- **Follow-through:** action items that keep rolling over signal overload, unclear priorities, or a blocker they're not naming.
- **Topic mix:** all tactics, no growth for several weeks means the career thread has gone dormant — reintroduce it.

## Career conversations (how to run)

Three dedicated sessions, 45–60 min each:

- **Life Story:** understand what motivates them and what shaped their choices. *Output:* 3–5 themes + motivation drivers.
- **Future Dreams:** list 3–4 plausible long-term visions (not a single "answer"). *Output:* 3–4 dreams + what's appealing about each.
- **Career Action Plan:** pick 1–3 growth bets and turn them into practice loops. *Output:* growth bets, projects to seek, skills to build, and monthly checkpoints.

## Wellbeing / recovery (boundaries)

Use energy/joy check-ins to spot risk, not to diagnose:

- If someone reports persistent burnout symptoms, encourage company support resources and professional care.
- If workload is the driver, adjust scope, priorities, or expectations — and write it down.

## Skip-levels (do / don't)

- **Do:** clarify purpose; protect the manager-report relationship; share themes, not quotes or names.
- **Don't:** fish for performance gossip; promise confidentiality you can't keep; bypass the manager on decisions.
