# Org Design Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and failure modes for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## What you're optimizing (and constraints)

- Org design is a trade-off engine. Make the trades explicit: speed vs coherence, autonomy vs consistency, innovation vs reliability.
- Prefer 3-5 principles over 10+. If everything is a principle, nothing is.
- Without success metrics you can't tell whether the change helped or hurt - name 1-3 before designing.

## Mapping the org as a system

- Most "slow" orgs are slow due to dependency chains and unclear decision rights, not because people aren't working hard.
- Model the system as "work flows + handoffs," not just reporting lines.
- Hunt for missing approvers and hidden constraints - the ones that appear late cause rework and morale damage.

## Posture: centralized <-> decentralized (Apple-like <-> Amazon-like)

- **More centralization** works when you need an integrated customer experience and consistent quality/brand decisions.
- **More decentralization** works when you can define clean interfaces and want parallel execution with fewer coordination meetings.
- Decentralizing without interfaces/standards fragments the product and duplicates work. Centralizing without capacity creates bottlenecks and slow decisions.

## Posture: functional <-> divisional/value-stream

- **Functional** alignment (organized by discipline) improves craft consistency and reduces "mini-companies" - useful when you need to go back to startup and simplify.
- **Divisional/value-stream** alignment improves end-to-end ownership and speed for distinct products or journeys, if you keep standards and shared platforms healthy.
- Switching models without also changing decision rights and operating cadence changes nothing.

## Generating options

- Always generate at least two options; with one, you're rationalizing, not designing.
- Every option should answer: what dependencies are removed? What decisions move closer to the work? What stays standardized vs varies?

## Deciding with a score + narrative

- Use the rubric as a forcing function, then write the decision narrative: why this option, why now, why not the others.
- Pick a fallback in case a key assumption fails (talent maturity, architecture constraints).

## Transition (sequence + safety rails)

- Minimize Day 1 churn: change structure and decision rights, but keep in-flight work continuous.
- Define the Day 1 operating model: who decides, what meetings exist, where truth lives.
- Prefer pilots when uncertainty is high; avoid serial reorgs.
- No comms plan means rumor fills the gap - write one.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Reorg as strategy substitute.** "Structure will fix it" when the real problem is an unclear strategy or a people-performance issue.
- **The pretty org chart.** A neat chart that ignores how work actually flows and where handoffs stall.
- **Moving dependencies, not removing them.** A redesign that reshuffles boxes but leaves the same coordination load.
- **Hybrid as ambiguity.** A "hybrid" model where no one can say who decides.
- **Posture swap with no mechanism change.** Going functional or divisional without touching decision rights or cadence, then wondering why nothing improved.
- **Big-bang with no continuity plan.** Changing everything at once with unclear interim ownership, so in-flight work stalls.
- **Manager-only layers.** Adding management layers detached from the craft, which slows decisions and hides who owns outcomes.
