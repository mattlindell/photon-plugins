# Difficult Conversations Playbook (Heuristics)

Topic-keyed heuristics, defaults, and anti-patterns for the branches in `../SKILL.md`. Adjust every default to your context.

## Anti-patterns (catch yourself)

- **Burying the lede.** Ten minutes of warm-up before the actual message reads as evasive and cruel. Lead with it, then pause.
- **Labels instead of behaviors.** "You're not a leader" / "you're unreliable" is a verdict, not feedback. Name the behavior, the time, and the impact.
- **Attributions dressed as feelings.** "I feel you don't care" is a story about them. Say the true feeling ("I was concerned") plus the observation.
- **Kitchen-sinking.** Saving up a pile of grievances for one meeting overwhelms and derails. Two well-chosen examples beat ten.
- **Debating reality.** Relitigating every point re-opens a decision that is already made. Acknowledge, then return to the core message.
- **Improvising policy.** Freelancing on severance, timelines, or process in a separation. Coordinate with HR; do not invent policy in the room.
- **Delivering hard news by text.** Email or group chat for a termination or a denial strips dignity. Private, in person, live.

## Classifying the conversation (which lane)

Sort the request into one lane -- it sets the spine and the boundary:

- **Behavior change** -- feedback / coaching. You want a specific behavior to shift.
- **Decision delivery** -- promotion denial, role change, reorg news. The decision is made; you are delivering it well.
- **Separation** -- layoff / termination. HR-gated; stop and confirm process before drafting.

If the request touches policy, legal risk, harassment, or an investigation, **stop** and route to HR/legal regardless of lane.

## Observations, not labels (facts vs stories)

Use **observation -> impact -> request** (or Situation -> Behavior -> Impact) to keep feedback specific and fair. Prefer 2-5 examples over a long list.

- Label (avoid): "You're not strategic."
- Attribution (avoid): "I feel like you don't care."
- Observation (use): "In the last 3 sprints, tickets X/Y/Z shipped 5-10 days late with no escalation. Partner teams missed their dates as a result. Going forward I need blockers raised the day they appear."

Separate your feeling from your story: "When \<observation\>, I felt \<feeling\>. I need \<request\>." A feeling is one word about you; a story is an accusation about them.

## The message map (the spine)

Build the conversation in this order, then script it:

1. **Opening + intent** -- direct and caring; set the tone in one sentence.
2. **Key message** -- the core truth in 1-2 sentences, no hedging.
3. **Evidence** -- 2-5 concrete observations.
4. **Impact** -- why it matters (team / customer / business).
5. **Ask / decision** -- what changes, or what is decided.
6. **Support** -- what help you will provide.
7. **Boundaries** -- what is not up for debate; what happens if nothing changes.
8. **Close** -- summarize and name the next step.

## Talk track + anchor phrases

- Write it the way you will say it: short sentences, no long explanations.
- Plan a **pause** right after the key message and let the silence do its work.
- Prepare 2-3 **anchor phrases** to repeat if they argue or negotiate:
  - "I hear you. The decision is \<X\>. Let's focus on next steps."
  - "I'm going to stay on the core message here."
  - "This isn't up for debate today; what I can help with is \<Y\>."

## Emotion handling (when it gets hard)

Default moves, in order:

1. **Pause** -- silence is allowed and often better than filling it.
2. **Name and acknowledge** -- "I can see this is upsetting."
3. **Listen and reflect** -- summarize what you heard.
4. **Clarify** -- ask one question.
5. **Return to the core message** -- do not debate reality.
6. **Next steps** -- what happens now, by when.

Acknowledge emotion without walking back the message. Empathy and firmness coexist.

## Hope + a path (decision denials)

For a promotion denial or disappointing decision, after the clear "no," add:

- "Here's what would need to be true for the next level" -- tied to the bar, not to character.
- "Here's the plan and how I'll support you."
- "Here's when we'll revisit."

Do not soften the decision into ambiguity to make the moment easier -- that only defers the pain and erodes trust.

## Logistics + dignity

- Private setting, adequate time, no interruptions.
- Video or in person for anything emotionally loaded -- never text.
- For layoffs/terminations, follow policy and coordinate with HR; you can be compassionate without improvising process.

## Follow-up system (make it real)

Feedback is a practice, not an event -- the follow-up matters as much as the script.

- **Behavior change:** send a summary within 24 hours (what you observed, what you agreed will change, support you'll provide, next check-in). Default cadence: weekly for 2-4 weeks.
- **Decision delivery (denial):** send a short recap plus growth-plan milestones and a review date.
- **Separation:** follow HR/policy for documentation and communications; keep the written record factual and minimal.

## HR lines (when to stop)

Hand off, do not draft specifics, when:

- The matter involves harassment, discrimination, threats, or any investigation.
- You need legal, severance, or policy interpretation.
- The decision itself (fire / promote / discipline) has not been made or confirmed.
- There is an active safety or mental-health crisis -- follow policy and point to professional resources.
